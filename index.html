Chapter 1: The Speed of Light is a Jerk

[SYSTEM LOG: 11:54 PM]

The problem with the speed of light is that it’s too slow.

That sounds like a "first-world space problem," but sit in my chair for ten minutes and you’ll agree. Earth is 384,400 kilometers away. Light takes roughly 1.28 seconds to get there. That means if I send a "Hello" to my mom in Sammamish, it takes 1.28 seconds to travel, she takes five minutes to ignore it, and her "Why aren't you a doctor?" response takes another 1.28 seconds to come back.

Total round-trip latency: 2.56 seconds plus maternal disappointment.

I spend my nights at the Lunar Gateway monitoring the laser-comms. It’s a boring job. I watch the Deep Space Network (DSN) pulses bounce off the lunar reflectors and head back to the Canberra station on Earth. It’s just photons moving through a vacuum.

Except tonight, the math is broken.

I ran a diagnostic on the Uplink-4 laser. The bounce-back should have been a clean 2.56214 seconds. Instead, the return ping came in at 2.56219.

That’s a difference of 50 microseconds. In the networking world, 50 microseconds is a lifetime. It’s the difference between a clean packet and a total system crash. If the distance hadn't changed and the environment hadn't changed, then the speed of light had to have changed. And since I’m pretty sure physics doesn't just "take a night off," there was only one other option.

Something was catching my data mid-flight.
