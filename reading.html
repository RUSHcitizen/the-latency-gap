Chapter 1: The Speed of Light is a Jerk

They told us the future would be instantaneous. They promised us a world where data moved like thought—weightless, immediate, and everywhere at once. But physics doesn't care about your marketing department's promises. Physics has a speed limit, and it's a cold, hard constant.

At 299,792,458 meters per second, light is fast, but in a world where financial trades are decided in nanoseconds and neural links require sub-millisecond synchronization, light is a crawl. When you're trying to bounce a signal from a lunar colony back to an orbital relay, that 1.282-second delay—the "Latency Gap"—is an ocean.

Elias sat in the dim glow of the terminal, watching the deviation ticker. A five-microsecond jitter shouldn't have mattered. In the old days, it was a rounding error. Today, it was the difference between a successful handshake and a catastrophic desync.

"Come on," he whispered, his fingers hovering over the mechanical keys. He wasn't just chasing data; he was chasing the ghost of a signal that shouldn't exist. "Bridge the gap, you bastard."

The screen flickered. For a split second, the latency dropped to zero. Impossible. Zero latency meant the data hadn't traveled the distance—it had simply been there.
